WHY TEAMS WOULD USE IT

Less chasing.
More certainty.

Designed for the packaging, quality and artwork people who give the final check.

01

Catch the small things

Quantities are compared after unit conversion, so 0.45 L matches 450 ml and 500 ml does not. Product codes, GS1 barcode check digits, required wording and allergen lists are checked by exact rules.

02

See the source of every finding

Each finding shows the approved value, the artwork value, the field it came from and why it was raised. Nothing depends on trusting a score.

03

Send corrections, not email chains

The artwork studio sees exactly what to change and replies in context. It does not see the rest of the review.

04

Know it was fixed

When a corrected version arrives, the same checks run again. A finding is marked fixed because the artwork changed, not because someone ticked a box.

05

Make decisions you can defend

Print decisions are tied to one artwork version, can require two different reviewers, and are written to an audit log and an exportable report.

06

Work to your own rules

Mark which fields are critical, require critical findings to be fixed rather than waived, and switch AI suggestions on or off for the whole workspace.

USE CASES

Where differences
tend to slip through.

Reformulation and size changes

A new fill size or recipe updates the specification. Check that every pack component carries the new quantity, code and barcode.

Supplier and co-packer artwork

Check incoming artwork from agencies, suppliers or customers against the approved specification before it moves toward print.

Allergen and warning updates

Make sure a newly declared allergen or a revised safety statement appears on every affected label, with nothing dropped.

Market and language variants

Review each market version against its own approved specification, with findings kept separate per variant.

Promotional and seasonal packs

Short-run designs change fast. Confirm the regulated details survived the creative changes.

Rebrands and range refreshes

When a whole range is redrawn, review many products against their specifications with a trail for each one.

SAFEGUARDS

AI that assists.
People who decide.

The prototype already enforces the review gates below. Security items describe the proposed production design.

A person decides

Proofloom never approves artwork. Print decisions are recorded by people, for one exact version, and can require two different reviewers.

AI is labelled and evidence-bound

AI suggestions are marked as such, must quote the source text they rely on, and need a reviewer to confirm or dismiss them. That gate cannot be switched off.

Exact checks stay exact

Quantities, codes, barcodes, allergens and required wording are checked by deterministic rules, so switching AI off never hides an exact mismatch.

No compliance claims

Proofloom assists review. It does not certify regulatory compliance or guarantee error-free packaging, and it says so in the product.

Your files stay yours

Proposed design: encryption at rest and in transit, per-customer isolation, and no use of customer files to train AI models.

Traceable and time-limited

Every action is written to an audit log. Administrators set retention windows for artwork, reports and logs.

Labelling and packaging rules vary by product category and market. Responsibility for meeting them stays with the brand owner and its reviewers.

PROPOSED PRODUCTION DESIGN

Architecture on
Amazon Web Services

The proposed architecture supports document comparison and review workflows. The interactive demo uses sample data.

  1. 01

    Document intake

    Amazon Cognito

    Authenticate reviewers before document intake.

  2. 02

    Versioned storage

    Amazon S3

    Store versioned specifications, artwork, and report exports.

  3. 03

    Extraction and checks

    Textract + Lambda

    Extract scanned text and tables; run exact quantity and code checks.

  4. 04

    AI-assisted comparison

    Amazon Bedrock

    Suggest semantic discrepancies using supplied source evidence.

  5. 05

    Human review

    DynamoDB + Lambda

    Store findings, assignments and decisions; coordinate processing and reports.

Exact checks ≠ interpretation. Lambda handles deterministic field checks. Bedrock assists with meaning and explanations; reviewers evaluate the evidence.

No AWS services are connected to this demo. Website hosting is a separate choice from the proposed SaaS infrastructure.

FAQ

Questions packaging
teams ask.

Does Proofloom approve artwork automatically?

No. It finds and explains differences. A person records the print decision for one exact artwork version, and your workspace can require a second reviewer to confirm.

What does the AI do, and what does it not do?

In the proposed product, AI helps with things rules cannot judge well, such as whether a reworded claim means the same as the approved one. Every suggestion is labelled, must cite the source text, and needs a reviewer decision. Quantities, codes, barcodes, allergens and required wording are checked by exact rules, not by AI.

Does it certify regulatory compliance?

No. Proofloom checks artwork against the specification you approved. Whether that specification meets the rules for your product and market remains your responsibility.

How are unit differences handled?

Quantities are converted before comparing, so 0.45 L and 450 ml match. A change of unit type, such as grams against millilitres, is flagged as critical.

Can the artwork studio see our whole review?

No. The studio role sees only the corrections sent to it, can reply in context, and can upload a corrected version. Administrators see aggregate counts, not review contents.

Are our files used to train AI models?

The proposed design uses Amazon Bedrock under terms that exclude customer content from model training, and sends only the text needed for a suggestion. This will be confirmed in the data-processing agreement.

Is the product available today?

Proofloom is in the building stage. The interactive prototype runs in your browser with fictional data; uploads, text extraction and AI comparison need the production backend described in the architecture section.
